NASA data behind the decision

Where the ridges are

LRO LOLA provides elevation grids. The presets combine 80 m and 240 m data. Custom locations use the 240 m grid.

Where Earth and Sun are

JPL Horizons provides positions every 10 minutes. The planner checks whether each body clears the ridge line.

What a clear view means

Earth visibility is geometric line of sight. Sun visibility is potential solar exposure. Radio and power performance require separate engineering models.

How accurate is this?

The Horizons check holds terrain fixed. Running the same 2 m demo coordinates through the custom grid and ray settings changed Malapert’s outage from 20.48 to 11.53 hours, and de Gerlache’s from 7.66 to 8.23 hours. The terrain settings matter far more than the direction calculation in this case.

See the tests and their limits

Each row covers 14–26 November 2026 UTC at ten-minute cadence. Malapert at 2 m is the reference observer for the body-vector build. De Gerlache uses separately queried observer coordinates. All queries use the same JPL ephemeris.

The 30-minute tie rule is a comparison convention, equal to three ephemeris steps. Use the height and terrain checks to gauge sensitivity beyond that rule.

Method, limitations and attribution

The solar collection point stays at 2 m. Near ties use the dataset’s 30-minute screening threshold. That threshold is a decision rule. The 240 m grid shifted one original Malapert outage estimate by roughly nine hours, and terrain cells cannot resolve meters of antenna movement.

The model uses body centers. Receiver availability, radio link margin, irradiance, panel orientation and battery performance require separate estimates.

From a skyline to a mission decision

Plan the first check-in

The 17 November case misses a two-hour check-in at both sites with a 2 m antenna and terrain enabled. The first modeled Earth view opens about 7.7 hours after touchdown at de Gerlache and 20.5 hours at Malapert. A communications planner could test a later touchdown or plan relay coverage.

Check the battery and relay separately

On the same day, Shackleton Rim has potential solar exposure throughout the model window while Earth stays hidden. Engineers can use the longest shadow spell to size energy storage and the Earth windows to check when they need a relay.

Extend the study

This bundle compares five presets over 36 days. To study seasonal changes, extend the Horizons inputs across several lunar months and recompute visibility using the same terrain, then add finer local elevation data where decisions change.
